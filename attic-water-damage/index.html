Attic Water Damage: What Cypress Homeowners Should Know

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. For Cypress homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • Sheathing with fuzzy growth, black or white, concentrated toward the eaves
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Lost Insulation Value

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Mold Under the Roof Deck

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

How We Fix It

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Wood

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Correct the Source

We point out the roof or ventilation problem that needs correcting and can share our findings with your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.
