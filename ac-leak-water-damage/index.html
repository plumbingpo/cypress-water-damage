Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Cypress

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ACSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
  • Poorly connected drain piping, which lets water out before it reaches the outlet.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Trim and Baseboards

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

Nearby Flooring

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

The Platform and Closet Walls

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • The number of days water kept dripping before the AC was turned off.
  • The location of the air handler, since attic, garage and closet leaks spread differently.
  • Whether the water is still clean or has to be handled as Category 2.
  • How badly the platform holding the unit is damaged.
