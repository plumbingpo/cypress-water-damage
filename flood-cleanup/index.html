Why Professional Flood Cleanup Matters

There is much more to a flood cleanup than removing the water you can see. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together
  • Roof runoff should leave the downspout far enough out that it cannot soak the foundation
  • Debris in a gutter or storm drain blocks proper flow, so clean them out regularly
  • Consider flood insurance even if you are not in a designated flood zone
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Store valuables and important papers off the basement floor in waterproof containers

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Standing water anywhere on your property, whatever the source
  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater
  • Heavy rainfall, a dead sump pump and water spreading across the basement
  • Seepage at door thresholds, window wells or cracks in the foundation
  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
